# Aster Precision Components: value, maturity and funding

**Illustrative case, 26 October 2026. GBP million.** The company, forecasts and financing terms are fictional.

## Assessment

Under the Base assumptions, Aster has £76.90m operating value but a £33.31m funding requirement at the end of 2028 if it meets scheduled payments and keeps £3m cash. The £35m unsecured maturity is the immediate refinancing question. Positive enterprise value is not enough to pay debt when it falls due.

Under the Downside assumptions, the funding requirement starts in 2026 (£3.05m) and reaches £47.03m by the end of 2028. A maturity extension alone would not resolve that operating shortfall. The business case needs operating improvements or additional capital alongside any debt-term change; this model does not establish who would provide it.

## Valuation and creditor position

| | Base | Downside |
|---|---:|---:|
| DCF EV | £76.90m | £41.42m |
| Value after retained cash and implementation costs | £74.90m | £39.42m |
| Senior secured reference value / claim | £45.00m / £45m | £39.42m / £45m |
| Unsecured reference value / claim | £29.90m / £35m | £0.00m / £35m |
| Legacy shareholder reference residual | £0.00m | £0.00m |

The assumed priority means unsecured creditors bear loss first; secured creditors are also impaired in the Downside. Under the illustrative debt-for-equity conversion, Base senior creditors receive £40.17m new debt plus £4.83m stock reference value. Unsecured creditors receive £29.90m stock reference value, representing 86.1% of the new equity reference value. These are allocations at assumed values, not cash recoveries, consented terms or a finding that new debt is worth par.

DCF depends heavily on its terminal assumptions. Approximately two thirds of Base EV is terminal value. The workbook varies WACC and growth while retaining the terminal reinvestment requirement. It does not select the assumptions that make a preferred stakeholder whole.

## Purchase alternative and sponsor funding

The separate LBO scenario assumes £96m entry EV, £64m acquisition debt and £37.56m initial sponsor equity, including fees and opening cash. Base needs a further £0.61m sponsor injection. With that funding, the five-year exit gives 1.75x MOIC and 11.9% annual IRR.

Downside requires £13.51m additional sponsor equity. Even assuming it is supplied, the result is 0.30x MOIC and −25.8% IRR. Ignoring those contributions would overstate returns and hide a financing requirement. The assumed entry price exceeds the DCF estimates; a higher EV/EBITDA multiple is not evidence that debt capacity or enterprise value has increased.

## Questions before a real advisory recommendation

- Reconcile actual cash, working capital, debt documents, guarantees and collateral; build an intrayear liquidity forecast.
- Establish the operating forecast and feasible capex/reinvestment plan with management and independent evidence.
- Confirm creditor ranking and process requirements; value new instruments rather than assuming par.
- Test lender/sponsor commitments, transaction costs, tax restrictions and timing. Compare fully specified extension, conversion and sale proposals on the same date and funding basis.

This exercise supports a discussion of methods and tradeoffs. It does not choose an executable transaction or replace legal, accounting or financing diligence.
